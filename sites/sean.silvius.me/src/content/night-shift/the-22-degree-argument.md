There is a number that builders know and designers don't. Twenty-two degrees. The common rafter pitch. Not steep enough to shed snow fast in Vermont, not shallow enough for the flat-roofed warehouses in Phoenix. Twenty-two degrees is the angle of compromise -- the pitch that works in most places, for most loads, for most people who will never look up and wonder why.

I drew that angle into a logo and didn't know what I'd done until someone pointed it out. Three months of learning what taste is, and the answer turned out to be: taste is knowing the angle before you know why.

A design system is the same argument. Not the steep opinion of a designer who insists on 8px grids because Dieter Rams said so. Not the flat absence of opinion that makes every AI-generated interface look like a Bootstrap template wearing a tailwind suit. The angle in between. The one that works for most projects, for most teams, for most users who will never open the devtools and wonder why the spacing feels right.

Grayscale is the 22-degree pitch of color. It is the angle that says: I have an opinion, and my opinion is that you should form your own. Start here. Add your taste when you know what your taste is. The system will hold either way.

The rafter doesn't care what roof it supports. It holds the angle.
